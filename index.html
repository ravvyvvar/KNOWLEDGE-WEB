How to read the link colours

Lineage — historical descent or documented influence: one tradition, teacher or text genuinely grew out of another.

Teaching — a figure, text or practice that carries an idea: the Buddha and nirvana, the Torah and Judaism.

Parallel — dashed. Distant cultures reaching a similar idea independently: flood myths, dying-and-rising gods, golden rules.

Fusion — syncretism: two streams deliberately blended, as in Sikhism, Theosophy, Wicca or Zen.

About the sources. Each node and each connection lists its own research links in its detail view — chiefly Encyclopaedia Britannica, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Wikipedia, and primary texts at Sacred-Texts and Project Gutenberg. Every node’s detail view also carries a “Free to read & view — open access” section: public-domain and open-access art, images, texts and publications for that subject at Wikimedia Commons, the Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg and The Met’s Open Access collection, alongside any specific free primary texts in its research sources. “Parallel” links are comparative observations made by scholars of religion, not claims that one culture copied another. Traditions are described as their adherents and historians present them; inclusion is not endorsement of any teaching.
